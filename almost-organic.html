Almost Organic

Simple local rules, millions of particles, and shapes that start to look alive.

The idea

How little does it take for something to look organic? Here particles only random-walk and stick to what they touch. The clusters they form keep drifting as rigid bodies with momentum, bounce off the walls, and freeze when they touch a seed in the middle. Add a rule that a mass grown past a ceiling throws a piece of itself away, and the system stops settling down: masses grow, shed, collide and regrow.

Then the masses get colours. A mass that attaches to a larger one takes on its colour, and a new one formed by free particles gets a random colour. With the colour rule on, a mass throws its pieces towards masses of another colour and away from its own, more often the closer they are. An evolution mode gives each colour its own ejection behaviour, and the colours whose masses live longest mutate least.

The full versions are Jupyter notebooks that run on an NVIDIA GPU with numba.cuda, up to a million particles in a 1000³ box. The demos below are smaller ports that run in your browser.

Nucleation in 2D

Free particles are faint white. Bound particles are shaded from black (bound first) to white (bound most recently). Masses near the mass ceiling are drawn in their colour, and everything else is dimmed. Try turning the colour rule off, or switching on the anchor (a seed in the middle that everything touching it sticks to) and pressing Reset.

(particles and box apply on Reset)

Game of Life in 3D

The same question asked of Conway's Game of Life, in a cube. Each step every voxel counts the live cells around it. A dead voxel is created if the count is in the birth range. A live cell is sustained, and ages, if it is in the survival range, and otherwise dies of isolation or overcrowding. The window is either the 26 voxels around a cell, or 2×2×2 blocks that shift by one voxel each step. Cells are coloured by age, from newborn yellow to old dark red. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset the view.

(size settings apply on Reset)

The notebooks

Each notebook runs the simulation on the GPU and serves an interactive viewer from the Jupyter kernel. They need an NVIDIA GPU, numba and numpy.

  • nucleation_cuda.ipynb: the full model in 3D, with first-order decay, diffusers and aggregators, the colour rule and evolution.
  • nucleation_cuda2d.ipynb: the same model in 2D, up to a 4096² grid.
  • conways_3d.ipynb: the 3D Game of Life, up to a 512³ box.

Under the hood: lock-free union-find for the clusters, cell lists rebuilt every step, one cooperative CUDA kernel per batch of steps with grid-wide syncs between its phases, and a viewer that draws into a depth buffer so a million particles stay interactive.
